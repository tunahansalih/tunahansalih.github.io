# Grounded, Front-Loaded Twelve-Week Interview Curriculum

- Candidate: Tuna Han Salih Meral
- Target: PhD-level Research Scientist, Research Engineer, and adjacent Applied Scientist roles
- Research cutoff: September 3, 2026, America/New_York
- Curriculum version: `rs-re-2026-09-grounded`

## Result

The interview-prep page is now a source-grounded execution system rather than a long reading syllabus or a bank of generated questions.

Its governing rule is:

> No generic technical exercise is invented.

Coding uses published MIT 6.006 assignments. Probability and statistics use Stanford CS109 problem sets. ML implementation uses Stanford CS231n and official PyTorch/JAX tutorials. Debugging, deployment, experiment tracking, and monitoring use Full Stack Deep Learning labs. Systems arithmetic uses NVIDIA's published worked examples. Research evaluation uses the NeurIPS checklist and Tuna's real papers or résumé evidence.

The page contains:

- 60 scheduled sessions over 12 weeks;
- 4,070 minutes, or 67.8 hours, of scheduled work;
- 24 reusable source-backed practice banks;
- 11 observable readiness gates;
- 37 external public exercises;
- 8 authoritative-reference sessions;
- 15 source-grounded simulations;
- dormant role and company adapters;
- 14-day, 7-day, and final-72-hour interview modes.

A simulation only re-performs a sourced exercise or defends candidate-owned work. It never introduces a manufactured coding problem, debugging file, system-design scenario, or research prompt.

## Front-loaded milestones

The first three weeks are deliberately different from a slow twelve-week ramp.

| Milestone | Operational meaning |
|---|---|
| End of Week 1 | Emergency baseline across opening, MIT coding, FSDL debugging, CS109 probability, NVIDIA arithmetic, and paper defense |
| End of Week 2 | Every major interview surface has been exercised and the Day-14 gate has no critical zero |
| End of Week 3 | Credible full-loop evidence across graph coding, profiling, experiment critique, GPU reasoning, and distinct RS/RE framing |

Calendar completion alone does not establish readiness. Day-14 readiness and Week-12 mastery require passing their named gates. A failed gate creates exactly one matched source-backed repair and a retest, not a growing backlog.

## Workload and interaction model

The calendar uses Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Saturday. Friday and Sunday remain unscheduled. Eleven weeks contain 340 minutes; Week 6 contains 330 minutes and deliberately reduces novelty.

Each session exposes only what is needed to start:

1. Open one named source or practice guide.
2. Perform the next visible action.
3. Use one focus timer.
4. Produce one named artifact.
5. Compare it with one observable finish line.
6. Stop, or use the 10-minute rescue version.

Full inputs, the remaining steps, notes, source ledger, rubrics, and adapters stay behind progressive disclosure. A missed session archives when the next week starts.

## Role coverage

The common core is intentionally large because public role descriptions do not support removing coding from RS preparation or research judgment from RE preparation.

The scheduled lane distribution is:

| Lane | Minutes |
|---|---:|
| Common core | 2,515 |
| Research Engineer emphasis | 940 |
| Research Scientist emphasis | 615 |

The RS emphasis tests hypotheses, evidence, novelty, falsifiability, research taste, and concise paper defense. The RE emphasis tests implementation, profiling, distributed execution, performance arithmetic, deployment, recovery, monitoring, and factual production-system defense.

## Readiness gates

Every gate uses a 0–2 behavioral rubric:

- 0: missing, wrong, unsupported, or incomplete;
- 1: viable with a meaningful gap, prompt, or timing miss;
- 2: correct, self-contained, evidence-backed, and within time.

The gates cover opening narrative, general coding, ML implementation/debugging, mathematics/statistics, research defense, experiment design, ML systems, research talk, RS judgment, RE judgment, and full-loop mastery. Correctness and factual ownership are critical dimensions and cannot be offset by presentation quality.

## Interview adaptation

No company adapter is active by default. Recruiter instructions determine the real stages, duration, coding environment, language, libraries, internet access, AI policy, research presentation, systems scope, and behavioral format. Unknown fields remain `UNKNOWN`.

When an interview is within fourteen or seven days, the matching sprint replaces the normal weekly schedule. It does not stack on top. During the final 72 hours, no new topic is introduced: re-run one failed high-impact gate, rehearse the opening and one project defense, confirm logistics, and stop.
